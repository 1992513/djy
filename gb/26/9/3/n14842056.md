<tr><td VALIGN=TOP width="626"><h2 align=center>“一分钱一分货”英语怎么说？说错容易得罪人</h2>
<img width="600" src="https://i.epochtimes.com/assets/uploads/2023/10/id14091647-shutterstock_1926342947-1.jpg" class="" data-thumbnail="true" />
<h6></h6>
<hr>“一分钱一分货”这句话，天天都用得上，英文怎么说？</p><h2><span style="color:#008000"><strong>最直接的一句：</strong><strong>You get what you pay for</strong></span></h2><div class=" lg:hidden"><div class="m-auto flex items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_M_article_336x280_2"><div id="DJY_M_article_336x280_2"></div></div></div><p>这是最直接、最口语的对应，字面就是“你付多少钱，就拿到多少东西”。</p><div class="hidden lg:block"><div class="m-auto items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden hidden md:flex djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_D_article_300x250_1"><div id="DJY_D_article_300x250_1"></div></div></div><p>例：Their service is cheaper, but the signal is patchy. You get what you pay for.<br/>（他们家便宜，可是讯号断断续续。一分钱一分货。）</p><p>但这句话有个语气陷阱，很多人不知道，它常有“早跟你说了吧”的味道。朋友刚买了便宜货、正在懊恼，你回一句You get what you pay for，听起来就是“活该，谁叫你贪便宜”。</p><p>要讲得温和一点，不妨改成第一人称说你自己：</p><div class=" lg:hidden"><div class="m-auto flex items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_M_article_336x280_3"><div id="DJY_M_article_336x280_3"></div></div></div><p>I&#x27;ve learned you get what you pay for.<br/>（我学到教训了，便宜真的没好货。）</p><p>I guess you really do get what you pay for.<br/>（看来一分钱一分货这话是真的。）</p><div class="hidden lg:block"><div class="m-auto items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden hidden md:flex djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_D_article_300x250_3"><div id="DJY_D_article_300x250_3"></div></div></div><p>After three cheap blenders in two years, I&#x27;ve learned you get what you pay for.<br/>（两年换了三台便宜搅拌机，我学到教训了。）</p><p>I used to buy the cheapest tires. Never again. You really do get what you pay for.<br/>（我以前都买最便宜的轮胎。再也不了，一分钱一分货是真的。）</p><div class=" lg:hidden"><div class="m-auto flex items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_M_article_336x280_4"><div id="DJY_M_article_336x280_4"></div></div></div><div><figure style="max-width:640px" class="aligncenter"><ahref="https://i.epochtimes.com/assets/uploads/2022/10/id13840523-shutterstock_1918035908.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><picture><source srcSet="https://i.epochtimes.com/assets/uploads/2022/10/id13840523-shutterstock_1918035908.jpg" type="image/webp"/><source srcSet="https://i.epochtimes.com/assets/uploads/2022/10/id13840523-shutterstock_1918035908.jpg" type="image/jpeg"/><img src="https://i.epochtimes.com/assets/uploads/2022/10/id13840523-shutterstock_1918035908.jpg" alt="购物时经常面临的抉择，真的是一分钱一分货吗？(Shutterstock)" width="640" b="auto"/></picture></a><figcaption>购物时经常面临的抉择，真的是一分钱一分货吗？(Shutterstock)</figcaption></figure></div><h2><span style="color:#008000"><strong>另外三种说法</strong><strong>?</strong></span></h2><h3><strong>1. Quality comes at a price.</strong><strong>（</strong><strong>好品质本来就要付代价</strong><strong>）</strong></h3><p>Verizon is more expensive, but quality comes at a price.<br/>（Verizon是要贵点，但好品质就是要付出代价啊。）</p><div class="hidden lg:block"><div class="m-auto items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden hidden md:flex djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_D_article_300x250_4"><div id="DJY_D_article_300x250_4"></div></div></div><p>The Japanese knife costs three times more, but quality comes at a price.<br/>（那把日本刀贵了三倍，但好东西就是要付代价。）</p><div class=" lg:hidden"><div class="m-auto flex items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_M_article_336x280_5"><div id="DJY_M_article_336x280_5"></div></div></div><p>Everything here is handmade, so it&#x27;s slow and it&#x27;s not cheap. Quality comes at a price.<br/>（这里全是手工做的，所以慢，也不便宜。好货自有代价。）</p><h3><strong>2. It&#x27;s a false economy.<strong>（<strong>这是假省钱<strong>）</strong></strong></strong></strong></h3><p>这个字组值得学。词典的解释是：一开始省了钱，长期算下来反而更花钱，明省暗贵。</p><p>Buying the cheapest printer is a false economy—the ink costs a fortune.<br/>（买最便宜的印表机是假省钱，墨水贵得吓人。）</p><div class="hidden lg:block"><div class="m-auto items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden hidden md:flex djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_D_article_300x250_5"><div id="DJY_D_article_300x250_5"></div></div></div><p>Skipping the annual service on your car is a false economy.<br/>（省下每年的定期保养，是假省钱。）</p><h3><strong>3. Penny-wise and pound-foolish.<strong>（<strong>捡了芝麻丢了西瓜<strong>）</strong></strong></strong></strong></h3><p>老一点的谚语，“省小钱，赔大钱”，适合用在提醒别人的时候。</p><p>He drives 20 miles to save 10 cents a gallon—penny-wise and pound-foolish.<br/>（他为每加仑省一角钱，开20哩去加油，捡芝麻丢西瓜。）</p><p>Skipping insurance to save a few dollars a month is penny-wise, pound-foolish.<br/>（为一个月省几块钱不保险，省小钱赔大钱。）</p><p>英国人日常经常说：Buy cheap, buy twice（买便宜的，就得买两次）。也是这个逻辑。</p><h2><span style="color:#008000"><strong>反义句：“便宜又大碗”怎么说？</strong></span></h2><p>有一分钱一分货，就有花小钱办大事。</p><h3><strong>1. Bang for your buck</strong></h3><p>词典解释为“花的钱换回来的价值”，也就是我们说的CP值。</p><p>This phone gives you the most bang for your buck.<br/>（这支手机CP值最高。）</p><p>If you want bang for your buck, buy last year&#x27;s model.<br/>（想要CP值，就买去年的型号。）</p><h3><strong>2. Value for money</strong></h3><p>英式用法，物有所值。</p><p>The lunch set is great value for money.<br/>（这个午餐套餐物有所值）</p><p>Is it good value for money?<br/>（这划算吗？）<strong>?</strong></p><h3><strong>3. Worth every penny</strong></h3><p>每分钱都花得值。</p><p>The extra $30 a month for reliable service? Worth every penny.<br/>（另外多花30刀买可靠服务？这钱值得花）</p><p>一分钱一分货，有时候买的并不是东西本身，而是别人塞车时你不必塞。</p><h2><span style="color:#008000"><strong>再加三句</strong></span></h2><h3><strong>1. What a rip-off!</strong><strong>（太坑</strong><strong>人</strong><strong>了）</strong></h3><p>$12 for a bottle of water at the airport? What a rip-off.</p><h3><strong>2. That&#x27;s a steal.</strong><strong>（太划算了）</strong></h3><p>$60 for two lines? That&#x27;s a steal.</p><h3><strong>3. You&#x27;re just paying for the name.</strong><strong>（你买的只是那个牌子）</strong></h3><p>用来吐槽名牌溢价，跟“一分钱一分货”刚好唱反调。@*</p><p><strong><em>作者简介：</em></strong><em>老哈利在中国大陆有超过</em><em>15</em><em>年的一线教学经验，曾在新东方英语等英语培训机构教授雅思、托福等课程，并创办自己的英语培训学校，所开设之“中老年实用英语班”广受学员欢迎。</em><em>移民美国后特在此开设“新移民实用美语”栏目，希望您在此学到英语教科书上不教的、免费英语班上外教不讲的一些最实用的零基础英语，助您尽快融入北美社区生活。</em></p><p>责任编辑：沈少棋
