<tr><td VALIGN=TOP width="626"><h2 align=center>老外为何祝你break a leg“摔断腿”？</h2>
<img width="600" src="https://i.epochtimes.com/assets/uploads/2021/08/id13186534-shutterstock_1904206021.jpg" class="" data-thumbnail="true" />
<h6></h6>
<hr>英语里有一个短语，用起来很诡异，但又是货真价实的地道口语：演出就要开场，后台一片忙乱，同伴拍拍你的肩，笑着撂下一句：“<strong>Break a leg!</strong>”——摔断一条腿？这是哪门子的祝福？</p><p>别慌。这恰恰是英美剧场里最郑重的一句吉利话：祝你演出成功、旗开得胜。只是它偏偏反着说。</p><div class=" lg:hidden"><div class="m-auto flex items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_M_article_336x280_2"><div id="DJY_M_article_336x280_2"></div></div></div><h3><span style="color:#800080">一句“反话”吉利话</span></h3><div class="hidden lg:block"><div class="m-auto items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden hidden md:flex djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_D_article_300x250_1"><div id="DJY_D_article_300x250_1"></div></div></div><p>道理藏在剧场一个古老的忌讳里：演员们深信，临上台前若直愣愣地祝人“Good luck（好运）”，反倒会招来霉运，把好戏说砸。既然吉利话犯忌，那就反其道而行——祝你“摔断腿”，盼的正是相反的结果。这套“说坏话、讨好彩”的心思，在西方剧场流传了近百年。</p><p>至于这句话究竟从哪儿来，却是一笔有名的糊涂账，众说纷纭。有个流传最广的说法是这样的：据说1865年，刺杀林肯总统（Abraham Lincoln）的凶手、演员出身的布思（John Wilkes Booth），行刺后从福特剧院（Ford&#x27;s Theatre）的包厢跳上舞台，正摔断了一条腿，“break a leg”便由此而来。故事讲得活灵活现，可惜站不住脚——这句话晚了几十年才出现，跟那桩血案并不相干。</p><h3><span style="color:#800080">剧场里的一堆忌讳</span></h3><div class=" lg:hidden"><div class="m-auto flex items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_M_article_336x280_3"><div id="DJY_M_article_336x280_3"></div></div></div><p>“break a leg”不是孤例。西方剧场的规矩多着呢：后台不许吹口哨；谁也不能在剧院里直呼《马克白》（Macbeth）的剧名，据说那会招灾，只能讳称“那出苏格兰戏（the Scottish Play）”。万一有人失口说了“good luck”，还得想办法破解。破解之法还挺繁琐：得走出剧院、原地转3圈、啐一口、骂一句，再敲门乞求别人邀请自己重新进剧场。</p><p>说到这儿，中国人该会心一笑。梨园行的忌讳一样不少，后台自有一套讲究，图的也是个吉利。东方西方的戏班，隔着半个地球，却不约而同地信着同一个理：好话不宜说尽，吉利要反着讨。</p><div class="hidden lg:block"><div class="m-auto items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden hidden md:flex djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_D_article_300x250_3"><div id="DJY_D_article_300x250_3"></div></div></div><h3><span style="color:#800080">这一簇“祝好运”怎么说</span></h3><p>“break a leg”虽好，到底是演出、上台、面试、演讲这类“要亮相、要表现”的场合才用。日常想祝人好运，还有一簇更顺口的：</p><div class=" lg:hidden"><div class="m-auto flex items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_M_article_336x280_4"><div id="DJY_M_article_336x280_4"></div></div></div><p>最平常的，就是<strong>“Good luck!”（祝你好运！）</strong>——注意，这句只是在剧场里犯忌，台下用却毫无问题。</p><p>想说<strong>“去把他们镇住、大放异彩”</strong>，用<strong>“Knock &#x27;em dead!”</strong>或<strong>“Knock their socks off!”</strong>。</p><p>盼事情顺遂，就把手指交叉：<strong>“</strong><strong>Fingers crossed!”（但愿如此！）</strong>／I&#x27;ll keep my fingers crossed for you.。</p><div class="hidden lg:block"><div class="m-auto items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden hidden md:flex djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_D_article_300x250_4"><div id="DJY_D_article_300x250_4"></div></div></div><p>给人壮胆打气，那句百搭的<strong>“You&#x27;ve got this!”（你没问题！）</strong>永远不会错，还有<strong>“You&#x27;ll nail it!”（你一定拿得下！）</strong>。</p><div class=" lg:hidden"><div class="m-auto flex items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_M_article_336x280_5"><div id="DJY_M_article_336x280_5"></div></div></div><h3><span style="color:#800080">Chinglish避雷</span></h3><p>千万别把“Break a leg!”当真，回一句“干嘛咒我摔断腿”，那就闹笑话了——它是地地道道的祝福。</p><p>场合要分清：朋友考试、面试、上台前，说break a leg很地道；可要是人家真要去做手术、或正身体不适，这句就万万使不得。</p><p><strong>来，练几句</strong></p><div class="hidden lg:block"><div class="m-auto items-center justify-center overflow-hidden print:hidden hidden md:flex djy-ad" style="min-height:266px;padding:8px 8px 8px 8px" data-testid="ad-container-DJY_D_article_300x250_5"><div id="DJY_D_article_300x250_5"></div></div></div><p>You&#x27;re on in five minutes—break a leg!（还有5分钟上场，祝你演出成功!）<br/>Big interview tomorrow? Break a leg—you&#x27;ve got this!（明天大面试? 祝你顺利，你没问题!）</p><h3><span style="color:#800080">English Corner</span></h3><p>break a leg （祝演出、上台）旗开得胜、马到成功（字面“摔断腿”，反话讨彩）<br/>Good luck! 祝你好运（剧场忌用，台下通用）<br/>Knock &#x27;em dead! / Knock their socks off! 去大放异彩、把全场镇住<br/>Fingers crossed! 但愿如此、求个好运<br/>You&#x27;ve got this! 你没问题！／You&#x27;ll nail it! 你一定拿得下！<br/>the Scottish Play （剧场行话）《马克白》的讳称? @</p><p>责任编辑：李琳#
